The first "as" introduces the quality being compared: "just as important."
The second "as" introduces what is being compared to it: "profit."
The structure "as...as" is a common way in English to show that two things are equal in some way. In this case, it means giving back is equally important as profit.

Why "as...as" is often used:
We use "as...as" to compare things that are equal or similar in a certain aspect. Some other examples include:

"She is as tall as her brother." (Both are equally tall)
"This task is not as difficult as it seems." (Comparing the difficulty)
"You can run as fast as I can." (Comparing speed)
