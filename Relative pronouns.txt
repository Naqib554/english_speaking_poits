Relative pronouns:

They introduce additional information about a noun or pronoun in a sentence. 
Let’s explore their functions with examples:


"who" and "whom" are both refers to people althow "whom" use as an object pronoun and "who" 
 use as a subject pronoun. 

Who:
Used for people.
Example: The person who won the award is my friend.

Whom:
Also used for people but is less common in modern, informal English.
Example: The doctor, whom I admire, is retiring.
Subject Pronoun: She hired the man who impressed her during the interview.
Object Pronoun: She hired the man whom she interviewed yesterda

Whose:
Indicates possession and can be used for both people and things.
Example: The boy whose bicycle was stolen is upset.


Which:
Used for things or animals.
Example: The book, which is on the table, belongs to me.

That:
Used for both people and things.
Common in informal language.
Example: The movie that we watched was fantastic.

Remember:
Use "who" for people.
Use "which" for things and animals.
Use "whose" to show possession(ownership).
Use "that" for both people and things, but it's more common in informal language.
"Whom" is less common and is usually used in formal writing or speech.
