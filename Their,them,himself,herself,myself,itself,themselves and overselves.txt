Difference between themselves and ourselves:
"Themselves" typically refers to a group of people where the speaker is not included.
"Ourselves" can refer to a group that includes the speaker, emphasizing their involvement in the action, or it can be used to emphasize the subject's 
involvement regardless of whether the speaker is part of the group.

Them:
The word "them" is typically used in conversation as a pronoun to refer to a group of people or things that have already been mentioned or are understood from context. Here are some common situations when you might use "them":

Referring to people previously mentioned:

"I met some new colleagues today. I introduced myself to them."
"My friends are coming over later. I'll introduce you to them."
Referring to objects or things:

"I bought some new books. Have you read them?"
"The dishes are clean. You can put them away."
Referring to unspecified people or things:

"Someone left their umbrella here. I wonder if they'll come back for it."
"I heard noises outside, but I couldn't see anyone. It might have just been them."
Gender-neutral pronoun:

"I have a new neighbor. I think I'll invite them over for dinner."
"Someone left their phone on the table. I hope they come back for it."
Emphasis or clarification:

"I didn't mean to upset them."
"It wasn't me who called them."
