**Interactive Lesson on Adjectives and Intensifiers**

---

### **Part 1: What is an Adjective?**

#### Definition:
An **adjective** describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. It tells us more about the quality, size, color, number, or condition of the noun.

#### Examples:
- *The car is **red**.* (Color)
- *He is a **tall** boy.* (Height)
- *She has **five** apples.* (Number)
- *The weather is **cold** today.* (Condition)

#### Activity:
**Identify the Adjective**
- The cat is black.
- They live in a big house.
- She has three pencils.

---

### **Part 2: Types of Adjectives**

1. **Descriptive Adjectives**: Describe qualities or characteristics.
   - *beautiful, happy, smart*

2. **Quantitative Adjectives**: Indicate quantity or amount.
   - *some, many, few, several*

3. **Demonstrative Adjectives**: Point out specific nouns.
   - *this, that, these, those*

4. **Possessive Adjectives**: Show ownership.
   - *my, your, his, her, our, their*

#### Activity:
**Match the Type with the Adjective**
- *His car is new.* (**Possessive**)
- *This book is mine.* (**Demonstrative**)
- *I ate some bread.* (**Quantitative**)

---

### **Part 3: Comparative and Superlative Adjectives**

#### Rules for Comparatives:
1. Add **-er** to 1-syllable adjectives.
   - *tall → taller*
2. Use **more** for 2+ syllable adjectives.
   - *beautiful → more beautiful*
3. Irregular adjectives change completely.
   - *good → better*

#### Rules for Superlatives:
1. Add **-est** to 1-syllable adjectives.
   - *tall → tallest*
2. Use **most** for 2+ syllable adjectives.
   - *beautiful → most beautiful*
3. Irregular adjectives change completely.
   - *good → best*

#### Special Case:
- If an adjective ends in **"y"**, change **"y"** to **"i"** and add **-est**.
  - *happy → happiest*

---

### **Part 4: What is an Intensifier?**

#### Definition:
An **intensifier** is a word used to strengthen or emphasize an adjective, adverb, or verb.

#### Examples:
- *She is **very** beautiful.*
- *This book is **so** interesting.*
- *The movie was **even** better than expected.*

---

### **Common Intensifiers**

1. **Very**: Used with most adjectives or adverbs.
   - *It is **very** hot today.*

2. **Much**: Used with comparatives or certain adjectives.
   - *He is **much** smarter than his brother.*

3. **Even**: Adds emphasis to something surprising or unexpected.
   - *She is **even** taller than her father.*

4. **Really**: Used for strong emphasis.
   - *The view is **really** beautiful.*

5. **Too**: Indicates excess.
   - *The bag is **too** heavy.*

#### Activity:
