and also the sentence which include any preposition that sentence never be changed to pasive voice.
for instance:he sleeps.
he goes to school.
the first sentence include intransitvie verb and the second sentence contains preposition.


3)Subject pronouns in active voice (e.g., I, you, he, she, it, we, they)
 change to object pronouns in passive voice (e.g., me, you, him, her, it, us, them).

4)Possessive pronouns in active voice (e.g., my, your, his, her, its, our, their) 
change to possessive determiners in passive voice (e.g., my, your, his, her, its, our, their).

5)Reflexive pronouns in active voice (e.g., myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves)
 remain the same in passive voice.

Transitive verb: A verb that requires an object to complete its meaning and make sense in a sentence.
 For example: "He eats pizza." (The verb "eats" requires an object, in this case, "pizza," to complete the meaning.)

Intransitive verb: A verb that does not require an object to complete its meaning and can make sense on its own in a sentence.
 For example: "She sleeps." (The verb "sleeps" does not require an object to complete its meaning.)






Present Simple:
Active: They clean the house every week.
Passive: The house gets cleaned every week.

Present Continuous:
Active: They are painting the room.
Passive: The room is getting painted.

Present Perfect:
Active: She has fixed the car.
Passive: The car has been fixed.

Past Simple:
Active: He got paid last Friday.
Passive: He was paid last Friday.

Past Continuous:
Active: They were repairing the roof.
Passive: The roof was being repaired.

Past Perfect:
Active: They had finished the work.
Passive: The work had been finished.

Future Simple:(we include the  word "be" in passive voice when change future simple active voice into passive voice)
Active: They will deliver the package tomorrow.
Passive: The package will be delivered tomorrow.

Future Perfect:
Active: She will have completed the task by Friday.
Passive: The task will have been completed by her by Friday.





Summary:
In passive constructions, you don’t say something; rather, others say something about you.
