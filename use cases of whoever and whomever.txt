
"Whoever" and "whomever" both refer to an unspecified person, but their usage depends on the grammatical role they play in a sentence:

Whoever is used as the subject of a sentence or clause. It performs the action.
Example: Whoever finishes first will win the prize.

Whomever is used as the object of a verb or preposition. It receives the action.
Example: Give the ticket to whomever you want.

To decide which to use, check whether the word functions as a subject (use "whoever") or an object (use "whomever") in the clause.
