In English conversation, "is" is used with singular subjects, while "are" is used with plural subjects. For example:
"He is going to the store."
"They are going to the store."


When referring to a collective noun or a group of people acting as a single unit, "is" is used.
Examples include team, committee, family, class, etc. So, when you're talking about the group as a whole, you use "is" instead of "are".

"The team is working hard to meet the deadline."
"The committee is discussing the new policy."
"The herd of elephants is moving towards the watering hole."
"The jury is deliberating on the verdict."
"The staff is preparing for the upcoming event."
"The audience is applauding the performance."
