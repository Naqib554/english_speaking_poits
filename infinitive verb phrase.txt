“infinitive verb phrase” or “to-infinitive”
It encompasses various situations where the base form of the second verb follows the first verb, such as expressing intention, permission, causation

1)Verbs of Perception and Causation:
After verbs of perception (such as “see,” “hear,” “watch,” “notice,” etc.) and causation (such as “make,” “let,” “have,” “help,” etc.), we often use the base form of the second verb.

Examples:
“She saw him run across the street.”
“They made her sing a song.”
“He helped me carry the heavy boxes.”

2)Verbs of Intention, Decision, or Plan:
After verbs expressing intention, decision, or plan (such as “want,” “decide,” “plan,” “intend,” “choose,” etc.), we use the base form of the second verb.
Examples:
“I want to travel to Japan.”
“She decided to take a break.”
“We plan to visit the museum.”

3)Verbs of Preference and Desirability:
After verbs expressing preference or desirability (such as “prefer,” “like,” “love,” “hate,” “wish,” etc.), we use the base form of the second verb.
Examples:
“He likes to read mystery novels.”
“She wishes to learn a new language.”
“They prefer to eat vegetarian food.”

4)Verbs of Permission and Obligation:
After verbs indicating permission (such as “allow,” “permit,” “let,” etc.) and obligation (such as “must,” “should,” “ought to,” etc.), we use the base form of the second verb.
Examples:
“The teacher allows students to use dictionaries during the exam.”
“You must wear a helmet while riding a bike.”

5)Parallel Actions:
When describing parallel actions (actions happening simultaneously), we use the base form for both verbs.
Example:
“She cooked dinner while he cleaned the kitchen.”
Remember that these guidelines provide a general overview, and there may be exceptions. Context and specific verbs play a significant role in determining whether the base form or another form of the verb is appropriate. If you encounter any specific verbs or situations, feel free to ask for clarification!
